A one-line deadpan weather forecast for "Deadpan Forecasts for Cities That Do Not Exist": "Tonight in Wobbleford, fog thick enough to lose a bus in — visibility down to one lamppost and a rumor." The joke is a straight broadcast line for a city that does not exist, with the absurdity hidden inside a measurement no real meteorologist would give: fog thick enough to lose a bus in, then visibility reported not in metres but in one lamppost and a rumor — a deadpan downgrade from a unit of distance to a unit of gossip, delivered as if it were a normal reading. The sting lands on the last word. One concrete picture, no clever twist to decode, no second sentence to explain it. It avoids the spent angles of the genre (raining frogs, generic "strange weather", a picnic cancelled) and bets on a single vivid image instead of a pile of absurdity.
